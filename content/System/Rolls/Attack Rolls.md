At some point you may fail to remain undetected, or your charisma wasn't as good as you thought. And you're going to need to fight your way out (or into) the situation.

First you're going to need to hit your target. You can do so by succeeding* an attack roll.

\*to various degrees of success
# So, Attack Rolls, huh?
And how they're a little different yet maybe familiar.

When attacking, we don't just swing at a Nightmare creature or Awakened and pray we hit. We aim for a certain area of them and only THEN we pray we hit.

Attack rolls work the exact same as [[Action Rolls]] as in you still roll duality dice and apply your experiences, advantages, aspect dice etc. You also get to add your [[Weapon Proficiency]] now. However what is most different is localized damage. Or part or limb damage if that makes more sense in your mind.  
## Localized? You mean my language?
No, not in your language. But based on where you aim. Enemy combatants will be easier to hit depending on where you try to hit them (and where from). They will also take less or more damage depending on where you hit them.

Their eyes? OUCH! But first TRY and hit it. Most beings will try their hardest to defend their head and eyes by dodging or blocking. Perhaps they'll sacrifice their arm to avoid decapitation!

Before rolling an attack roll (not after!!) you may decide on where to hit.

# Bullseye! But at what DCost?
Your GM will decide how hard a monster is to hit. That will be their base DC. Like Evasion but for baddies with toothy maws. Then they'll assign various parts to be easier to hit or harder increasing or decreasing that base DC. 
## Situational
Keep in mind it's not only based on the difficulty of their body parts, but also on your position, situation and more. Hitting somethings head will be easier when your beefy ally is holding it in place. Or perhaps it's once hard to hit neck is now easy as you're climbing on it's back (so long as you hold on)! But this also means that once on it's back you cannot hit its potentially vulnerable belly.
## Localized Damage
Earlier I mentioned localized damage. This was intentional as damage also depends on where you hit it. Lets imagine a giant armadillo. But, like, corrupted with spikes and evil and not cute.

Hitting it on it's back might be easy, but it would also have an incredibly high damage resistance to body damage. Another part of localized damage is that monsters will no longer be one giant bar of hit points. Smash a creatures head in and it dies. Rip it's wings and prepare for a fall! (You should really get of its back at some point)

Injuries matter (for opponents I haven't decided on if I want to make you suffer yet)
## Be smart. Be safe. Be the last one standing, together
With your allies!

As mentioned earlier difficulty is situational. Create opportunities! Distract a hostile whilst your ally flanks it to hit its glowing crystals on its back! Lead a monster into the quick sand you totally found on purpose earlier.  Be smart, be creative. Think about what YOU want to do first before you think about the dice. Your GM will tell you when and if you'll need them.

# What if I Miss?
If you decide to try and hit a hard to hit a spot but don't make the DC. Then your GM would decide how the creature would prevent the hit. Would it have dodged? Would it have simply resisted the hit?

Was it close? Then your GM might decide that it blocked by sacrificing it's paw, or tilted its head so it hit it's shoulder. Nightmare spell is a dynamic TTRPG focused on story telling. Not an excel sheet where only numbers matter. Someone should've told this to the guy who wrote a whole bot just to calculate those numbers easier huh? 

Rule of thumb: if you miss a harder to hit part, but your roll was high enough to still hit the 'base DC' of the Nightmare creature, your GM will let you hit, graze or otherwise still attempt to wound  another (probably more armoured) part of the creature. So you can still succeed*

\*to various degrees of success

### Hope and Fear
Hope and Fear still come into play. A successful hit with Fear might result in a counter attack. Or a failure with Hope could grab the monsters attention and give someone else a great opportunity. If you want to read more about this, go and read the [[Combat Action Economy]] chapter.
# I can only hit one enemy per Attack?
Yes and no, this depends on the [[Memories|Memory]] type you are using and of course the  memories Attributes.

However in general if you would be able to within reach slash or smash multiple enemies you can. A great axe can cleave everything in range in front of you. Granted you roll high enough on your attack roll.
## Cleaving
To land multiple hits describe how you want to cleave and where, then roll as normal, just once. Then let's say you rolled a 21 to hit, not shabby at all. Then if you were trying to hit 3 humans in the chest each a DC of 10 then you'd be able to hit the first two but not the third. The math on that is at the end of this section.

Cleaving is not just limited to multiple enemies. Want to slice both tendons from behind? You can. Want to extend a slash from a wolves back to their paw? You monster that's just a poor animal! But you could!

You might wonder 'What is the point of stabbing then, are rapiers and stilettos just shit?' First of why are you using high heels to stab people? And second let me introduce you to Flurries! The stabbing not furry kind. Though I do know someone that would actually make a stabbing furry character.
#### Cleaving Math (and flurry but just one body part)
1st enemy: 21 > 10 -> hits and roll has 11 left (21 - 10)
2nd enemy: 11 > 10 -> hits and roll becomes 1
3rd enemy: 1 < 10 -> get's blocked / dodged
# Flurries
When using a stabbing weapon, which is any weapon with a pointy tip that you can convince your GM can be stabbed in rapid succession, have a flurry mechanic. When attacking a spot with a stab any 'overkill' to hit attack roll turns into a Flurry! See the Cleaving Math part above here for the math, except that only the same location can be targeted except multiple!
# Dual Wielding
I have two daggers, now what? Generally you can attack once per limb wielding a weapon. So two daggers means two attacks.
