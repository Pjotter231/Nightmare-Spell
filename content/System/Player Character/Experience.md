Experience can be seen as a special trait, skill or distinction. 

A character starts with 2 experiences that they think their character would have based on their characters life so far, gaining 1 extra experience for every rank they rise. And experience is a phrase or short description of what truly makes that character special.

Experiences are truly linked to a characters backstory. They aren't simply numeric increases to one mechanic. They cannot be too broad or grant mechanical benefits, and aren't recommended to be too specific either.

Some examples of too broad ones would be "Talented", "Skilled", "Lucky". As they can be applied to pretty much any situation. Likewise, "Flight" or "Invisible" are more alike to aspects than experiences. One cannot have simply 'Learned how to fly' as a human.

Experiences can however be very straight forward. "Singer" or "Bodyguard" are perfectly fine experiences. They can be flavoured to be more fitting of your character however. Such as "Nightingale" or "The wall". Both referring to talent of singing and the ability to not let people pass but also allowing for more creative application. 

Other examples would be "Never again" which could refer to losing someone in their backstory. Or "Ticking time" which would apply when in a hurry. 

So long as the experience can be linked back to the character and your DM agrees it's a fine experience.
## Bonuses
Experiences come in two forms. +2 and +1. You start of with one +2 and one +1 experience.
### Heads up
If you consistently use the same Experience on every roll, the GM will likely ask you to narrow the scope of that Experience or change it altogether. Experiences are meant to reflect the way your character has specialized in something important, not give you a bonus to all your actions. Creativity to how to apply your experience will be rewarded, but don't push it on top of every roll.

For example if you really need to succeed the first time you might convince the GM to use "Never again", as to not have to do it again. However applying this to every roll will become repetitive and over used.
